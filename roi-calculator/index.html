How the ROI calculator works

  1. Cost. Your monthly plan for the time frame you choose, plus the Website Buildout if you add it.
  2. Value of one new customer. Your price, any repeat business or referrals, and your gross margin give the profit one new customer brings you.
  3. Break-even. The cost divided by that profit is the number of new customers you need. Divided by your conversion rate, it becomes the number of leads, inquiries or visits you need.
  4. Timing. Nothing is counted until inquiries start and your usual sales cycle has run, so the work has to pay back in the months that remain.
  5. Reality check. The break-even number is set against the extra work you can take on and the customers you think you can win. A plan only makes sense if both have room for it.

What the result tells you

If break-even uses less than half of your spare capacity and your market estimate, the plan has room to pay for itself several times over. If it uses most of either, the plan can work but leaves little margin for a slow start. If it goes past either limit, a smaller plan, a longer time frame or more capacity will change the picture, and that is worth knowing before you spend anything.

Questions about marketing ROI

Why does the calculator use gross profit instead of revenue?

Revenue overstates the return. A $10,000 job with a 40% margin leaves $4,000 to pay for marketing, so that is the number that has to cover the plan.

How long does SEO take to pay for itself?

Most SEO programs take four to six months to show significant results, and businesses with long sales cycles, such as real estate or weddings, see revenue later still. That is why you can set when inquiries start and how long a sale takes. A 24- or 36-month view usually tells a fairer story than 12.

Should I use first-year or lifetime profit?

Start with first-year profit. If the plan pays for itself on that basis, repeat business and referrals are upside. Lifetime profit is useful when customers stay for years, as homeowners, members and advisory clients often do.

What if the result says the target is beyond my limits?

It usually means the plan is too large for your current capacity or market, not that marketing can't work for you. Try a smaller plan or a longer time frame, or bring the numbers to a strategy call and we'll look at where the gap is.

Are these numbers a forecast?

No. They show what would have to be true for the work to pay off, using numbers you enter. We don't promise rankings, AI mentions or sales. On a free strategy call we test your assumptions against search demand and competition in your market.
