How the ROI calculator works

  1. You pick your business. We fill in typical numbers for that kind of business, so you see an answer straight away.
  2. You pick a plan and a time frame. The cost is the monthly price times the number of months, plus the Website Buildout if you add it.
  3. We work out what one new customer is worth. That is the profit you keep from one new customer, not the total sale.
  4. We divide the cost by that profit. The answer is how many new customers pay for the plan. Using the share of inquiries that become customers, we then show how many inquiries that takes.
  5. We build in the wait. Digital marketing strategies typically take six months or more to mature and pay off, and some sales take months to close. We count a customer only after inquiries have started and a typical sale has closed, so the plan has to pay for itself in the months that remain.
  6. We check that it is realistic. We compare the answer with how much extra work you can take on and how many customers you could win. A plan only makes sense if there is room for both.

What the result means

Well within your limits means paying for the plan takes less than half of the extra work you can take on and less than half of the customers you could win. Within your limits, but tight means it takes more than half of one of them, which leaves little room if results arrive slowly. Beyond your limits means the plan needs more than you said you can handle or win. A smaller plan, a longer time frame or more capacity changes that, and it is better to know before you spend anything.

Questions about marketing ROI

Which numbers should I use?

Use your own wherever you know them, such as your average sale, your profit and the share of inquiries you turn into customers. Where you don't, keep our typical figures and see how the answer changes when you adjust them.

Why does the calculator use profit instead of sales?

Sales overstate the return. A $10,000 job with a 40% profit share leaves $4,000 to pay for marketing, so that is the number that has to cover the plan.

How long does SEO take to pay for itself?

Digital marketing strategies typically reach maturity and pay off after six months or more. Rankings and authority take time to build, and businesses with long sales, such as real estate or weddings, see revenue later still. That is why you can set when inquiries start and how long a sale takes. A 12-month view is the strictest test. A 24- or 36-month view shows what the strategy can do once it has matured.

Should I count repeat business and referrals?

Start without them. If the plan pays for itself on first-year profit alone, repeat business and referrals are a bonus. Turn them on when your customers stay for years, as homeowners, members and long-term clients often do.

What if the result says the plan is beyond my limits?

It means this plan asks for more than your business can handle or win today. A smaller plan, a longer time frame or more capacity changes that. Bring your numbers to a strategy call and we'll find where the gap is.

Is this a forecast?

No. It is not a forecast or a guarantee. It shows what would have to be true for the work to pay off, using the numbers you enter. No one can promise rankings, AI mentions or sales. On a free strategy call, we test your numbers against real search demand and competition in your market.
