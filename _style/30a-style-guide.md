# 30A Digital Marketing Style Guide

Version 1, November 1, 2026. Built from the Fluent Conveyors guide, the Oct 31 site audit, and the copy conventions in the build notes. Where this guide disagrees with an older project file, this guide wins.

## How to Use This Guide

Use it when writing or editing any copy for 30adigitalmarketing.com: pages, blog posts, emails, directory listings, and social profiles. Run `lint_30a.py` on every draft before it goes live (see the pre-publish checklist). The linter has two levels: **FAIL** means a hard rule is broken, and **REVIEW** means a person decides.

## Brand Overview

30A Digital Marketing is a remote-run growth marketing agency for established businesses on Scenic Highway 30A and across the Gulf Coast. We lead with organic search, AI search visibility, and off-site authority. Competitors in the area lead with photo and video, paid ads, or low-cost packages.

- **Legal name:** 30A Digital Marketing (footer: 30A Digital Marketing LLC).
- **Offers:** three monthly plans (Lantern, Beacon, and Lighthouse), the one-time Website Buildout, and Compass, a self-run SEO workflow.
- **Tagline:** Be found. Be chosen.
- **Service area wording:** Gulf Coast. National brands are welcome, with the focus on the Gulf Coast.

## Audience

Owners of established businesses who have built a good name and depend on being found and chosen online. They are often affluent, Southern, and somewhat conservative. They value their good name, straight answers, respect for their time, and having nothing changed without their approval.

- **Primary:** vacation rental companies, luxury real estate, hotels and resorts, wedding venues, golf courses, family entertainment, lifestyle brands, wellness and aesthetics practices, professional services, and home services.
- **Not a fit:** businesses that depend on government or military contracts, and businesses that cannot support $4,000 or more a month for the monthly plans (point them to Compass).

## Voice and Tone

Calm, evidence-led, courteous, and plain. We write as "we."

- Lead with specifics and numbers once we have real ones.
- Explain what a price buys. Never talk down to a reader who is watching their budget.
- AI is a tool under a person's control. It is never the selling point.
- No dialect and no folksy wording.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- FAQs are more conversational than page copy.
- Never invent results, client names, or statistics. Market figures come from sources we retrieved and list on the page.
- Say plainly that search strategies mature after six months or more.

### Remote Work

We are remote. Never imply an office, an address, or a local presence. The approved framing: "We don't keep an office on the coast, so your fee goes into the work rather than overhead." Never mention travel freedom.

### Things We Never Say

- Guarantees of rankings or AI mentions. A sentence that denies a guarantee is fine.
- "We don't do" video, photography, or influencer work. We stay silent about what we do not offer.
- Competitor-style claims such as "#1 agency" or "award-winning."

2. Plain pricing and no binding contract

4. Experience growing regional and national brands
5. Work scoped to each business, with the owner approving every change

## Mechanics

### Title Case for Headings

All headings use title case: H1, H2, H3, front matter titles, card titles, and button text that works as a heading. We follow Chicago style:

- Capitalize the first and last word.
- Capitalize nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and subordinating conjunctions of any length. That includes Our, Is, Be, We, Your, If, and Than.
- Lowercase articles (a, an, the), coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet), and all prepositions, whatever their length (about, from, with, into, through, between).
- Lowercase "as" and the "to" in an infinitive.
- Capitalize the first word after a colon.
- Hyphenated words: capitalize each main part (Month-to-Month, On-Page SEO).
- Keep acronyms in capitals: SEO, AI, ROI, FAQ, DIY, PPC, GEO, AEO.

Example: "What Our SEO Services Include" and "Common Questions about Destin SEO."

Sentence case stays for body copy, front matter descriptions, ledes, blurbs, and FAQ questions. A statement set as a display heading (a full sentence or two) also stays in sentence case, as on the homepage. The tagline keeps its own form: "Be found. Be chosen."

### Oxford Commas

Use a comma before the final "and" or "or" in a list of three or more, headings included: "search, AI visibility, and reputation." Pairs take no comma, and neither do two verbs sharing a subject ("we review the page and adjust the plan"). The linter flags likely misses, and about one flag in five is a false alarm, so a person decides.

### Money and Numbers

- Dollar amounts with a comma at $1,000 or more: $4,000, $7,000, $10,000. Never $4k or $4000.
- Ranges use an en dash with no spaces: 3–5 months, $4,000–$10,000.
- Per-month pricing reads "$4,000 a month."
- Numerals for figures and measurements. Spell out one through nine in running prose, except with units or percentages.

### Dashes

No em dashes anywhere. Use a comma, colon, period, or parentheses. No spaced hyphens used as dashes.

## Words and Constructions to Avoid

### Hard Bans (FAIL)

These break the guide. Rewrite them.

- **Clichés:** "it's recommended to," "designed to," "keep in mind," "it's important to note," "it's important to remember," "let's see," "when it comes to," "this article," "fear not," "can help to," "in today's," "the future of," "revolutionary," "think of it as," "picture a," "imagine a."
- **Openers:** "Enter" at the start of a sentence, "Here's the thing," "But here's."
- **Rhetorical setups:** "The result?" "The reality?" "The catch?"
- **False negatives:** "It's not X, it's Y," "It's not just X, it's Y," "This isn't just about X, it's about Y." Lead with what it is.
- **Fake experience:** "I've seen this play out," "I see this constantly."
- **From the Oct 31 audit:** "ambitious," "growth partner," "AI-era," "remote-native," "synergize," "compounds," "is only the beginning," "starts at the solution."

### Flags to Review (REVIEW)

These are fine in the right place. The linter flags them and a person decides.

- **consider, considered, considering:** fine when you want the reader to think something over. Not fine when the reader should make a choice. Use "choose" or "decide" there.
- **traditional:** fine when it names a real thing, such as traditional media.
- **shift:** fine when literal, such as an algorithm update.
- **A sentence that opens with "Most":** name who, or give a number.
- **guarantee:** fine only when denying one.
- **Plan-name capitalization and office or local-presence wording:** see the sections above.

### Structures to Avoid

Check every paragraph for these. The linter catches only the obvious forms.

- **Anaphora:** stacked sentences with the same opening ("more A, more B, more C").
- **False negatives:** stating what something is not before stating what it is.
- **Reversal framing:** "Most owners are drowning in data and starving for clarity."
- **Staccato:** clipped three-beat fragments ("No downtime, no rework, no delays").
- **Rhetorical questions** in running prose. Questions are fine as FAQ questions and in the fit quiz.

### The Tagline Exception

"Be found. Be chosen." is the brand line and is exempt from the staccato rule. Use it as written, with "Be chosen." in italics on the homepage. The pattern stays banned everywhere else.

## Page Conventions

- **Title tag (`seo_title`):** 65 characters or fewer, including " | 30A Digital Marketing."
- **Meta description:** 110–160 characters.
- **Primary keyword** from the page brief: in the title tag, H1, first sentence, one H2, and one FAQ question. Related keywords appear once each, where natural.
- **Opening paragraph:** short, quotable, and answer-first.
- **No closing call-to-action section.** The page template adds the strategy-call band. The homepage has its own.
- **Lead-generation pattern:** name what the free strategy-call audit covers for that page, add a "fit" section, and end with plain FAQs.
- **FAQs:** bold question, then a direct answer of 15–75 words.
- **Internal links:** follow SEO best practice. Write so that relevant pages earn a natural link: use descriptive anchor text that names the destination, link where it helps the reader decide or learn, and keep every address valid. There is no fixed link count. The front matter `related` list names the pages a reader should see next.
- **Sources:** list where figures come from. Paraphrase quotes and keep any quote under 15 words.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

1. Run `python3 lint_30a.py draft draft.md --repo <site folder>`, or `page` for a file already in the site. Fix every FAIL.
2. Read each REVIEW finding and decide. A flag that stays should be there on purpose.
3. Confirm no banned structure carries a hook, transition, or close.
4. Confirm headings are in title case (Chicago), and every list of three or more has its Oxford comma.
5. Confirm there are no invented results, no guarantees, and no hint of an office.
6. Read it aloud once. It should sound like a courteous expert who respects the owner's time.

## Linter Notes

- **Where it lives:** `_style/lint_30a.py` in the site repository (Jekyll does not publish folders that start with an underscore), with this guide beside it.
- **What it reads:** the site's own page files and front matter. No spreadsheet or cache step.
- **What it does not cover yet:** the quiz and calculator text built by JavaScript in `assets/js/site.js`, and the shared menu and footer in `_includes/`. Read those by hand.
- **Known false alarms:** Oxford flags on pairs and clause lists; keyword flags on brand terms and on pages that deliberately skip the keyword (such as the Approach page, which does not target "seo strategy").
- **FAQ answers:** 15–75 words each.
